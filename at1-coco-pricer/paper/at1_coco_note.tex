\begin{abstract}
We price a perpetual, non-cumulative Additional Tier~1 (AT1) instrument by
taking the issuer's Common Equity Tier~1 (CET1) ratio as the single state
variable. Every AT1 contingency---mechanical loss absorption, resolution
(Point-of-Non-Viability), coupon cancellation via the Maximum Distributable
Amount, and issuer call/extension---is a functional of that ratio, so a
capital-driven Monte-Carlo delivers mutually consistent trigger, coupon and
extension risk from one simulation. We describe the dynamics, the cash-flow
map, the risk-measure computation, and the calibration caveats.
\end{abstract}

\section{Instrument}
An AT1 CoCo is a perpetual, deeply subordinated bank instrument. It pays a
fixed coupon to a first call date (typically five years), after which the coupon
resets to a reference rate plus a contractual spread. Three features make it not
a bond:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Loss absorption.} If CET1 falls to a contractual trigger
  $c_{\text{trig}}$ (5.125\% or 7\%), the principal is written down or converted
  to equity. Resolution authorities may also impose loss at the Point of
  Non-Viability (PONV) before any mechanical trigger.
  \item \textbf{Coupon cancellation.} Coupons are discretionary and
  non-cumulative. Once CET1 enters the \emph{combined buffer requirement},
  CRD~IV caps distributions through the Maximum Distributable Amount (MDA).
  \item \textbf{Extension risk.} The instrument is perpetual; the investor
  relies on the issuer's economic incentive to call. Weak issuers---for whom
  refinancing is expensive---rationally extend, precisely when the investor
  least wants them to.
\end{enumerate}

\section{State dynamics}
Let $C_t$ denote the CET1 ratio in percentage points. Under the pricing measure
we model
\begin{equation}
  dC_t = \kappa(\theta - C_t)\,dt + \sigma\,dW_t - J_t\,dN_t,
\end{equation}
where $N_t$ is a Poisson process with intensity $\lambda_J$ (stress events) and
each jump $J_t>0$ is lognormal with median $m_J$ and log-volatility $\sigma_J$,
representing a \emph{downward} capital shock. Mean reversion to the target
$\theta$ stands in for management's capital planning and retained earnings;
upward jumps are deliberately absent, since recapitalisation is gradual whereas
losses are sudden.

Two absorption times are recorded per path: the mechanical time
$\tau_m=\inf\{t: C_t \le c_{\text{trig}}\}$ and an independent PONV time with
constant hazard $\lambda_{\text{PONV}}$. Absorption is
$\tau=\min(\tau_m,\tau_{\text{PONV}})$.

\section{Coupon cancellation via the MDA}
Let $b$ be the MDA floor (total capital requirement) and $w$ the combined-buffer
width. The maximum distributable fraction follows the CRD~IV quartile ladder in
the buffer $[b,\,b+w]$:
\[
  \phi(C)=
  \begin{cases}
    1.0 & C \ge b+w,\\
    0.6 & C \in [b+0.75w,\,b+w),\\
    0.4 & C \in [b+0.50w,\,b+0.75w),\\
    0.2 & C \in [b+0.25w,\,b+0.50w),\\
    0.0 & C < b+0.25w.
  \end{cases}
\]
The coupon paid on a live date is $\phi(C_t)$ times the scheduled coupon. This
makes coupon-cancellation risk endogenous: it emerges from the same paths that
generate trigger risk, and a \emph{wider} buffer requirement raises the hurdle
for full coupons---an adverse feature for the holder, recovered as a negative
buffer sensitivity in \S\ref{sec:risk}.

\section{Call and extension}
At each call date the issuer compares the cost of keeping the (reset) coupon,
$s_{\text{reset}}$, with the spread at which it could reissue,
\begin{equation}
  s_{\text{refi}}(C) = s_0 + \beta \max\bigl(0,\;(b+w) - C\bigr),
\end{equation}
and calls iff $s_{\text{reset}} > s_{\text{refi}}(C)$. Because
$s_{\text{refi}}$ \emph{widens} as capital erodes, a distressed issuer faces a
prohibitive refinancing cost and extends---the empirically observed behaviour
and the source of extension risk in the valuation.

\section{Valuation}
The price is the risk-neutral expectation of discounted cash flows under a flat
continuously-compounded curve $r$:
\begin{equation}
  V_0 = \mathbb{E}\!\left[
    \sum_{t_i < \tau \wedge \tau_c} \phi(C_{t_i})\,c(t_i)\,e^{-r t_i}
    \;+\; \mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_c \le \tau\}} F\,e^{-r\tau_c}
    \;+\; \mathbf{1}_{\{\tau < \tau_c\}} R\,F\,e^{-r\tau}
  \right],
\end{equation}
where $\tau_c$ is the (rational) call time, $F$ the face, $R$ the recovery on
absorption, and $c(t_i)$ the scheduled coupon (initial before the first call,
reset thereafter). Paths surviving to the horizon receive an analytic perpetuity
tail $c_{\text{reset}}F/y$ at a tail yield $y$, immaterial once the horizon is
long relative to $r$.

\section{Risk}\label{sec:risk}
Sensitivities are computed by central-difference bump-and-reprice under
\emph{common random numbers}: the same primitive draws price the base and bumped
scenarios, cancelling Monte-Carlo noise so the finite difference is stable at
moderate path counts. The engine reports $\partial V/\partial C_0$ (capital
delta), $\partial V/\partial\sigma$ (vega), sensitivities to $\lambda_J$ and
$\lambda_{\text{PONV}}$, the discount-rate DV01, and sensitivities to
$c_{\text{trig}}$ and the buffer width. It also returns the cumulative
absorption term structure $\Pr(\tau\le t)$ and the loss distribution
(expected loss, VaR, ES) of the discounted payoff.

\section{Calibration caveat}
CET1 is not a traded asset, so the state dynamics cannot be calibrated by
replication alone. In practice one anchors $\theta,\sigma,\lambda_J$ to the
issuer's capital plan and stress history (a real-world view), then adjusts the
hazards and recovery so that model prices match observed AT1 spreads and call
behaviour (a pricing view). This measure change is an identification gap, not a
free parameter: it should be stated, stress-tested, and---ideally---disciplined
by a cross-section of the issuer's instruments. The companion code ships with a
synthetic ground-truth generator so every estimator is checked against a known
answer before any market calibration.
